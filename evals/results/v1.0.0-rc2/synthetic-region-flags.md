# CV review: Senior Java Engineer for employers in Germany and Austria (DACH), visa sponsorship needed

**Verdict:** Borderline

**10-second verdict:** The top of page one shows "Senior Software Engineer (Java)", a most recent role at a bank with "Java 17 and Spring Boot" and "2 million transactions a day", and 10 years of experience: the core stack is a clear match, so a screener has a reason to keep reading (SCR-2, SCR-7, SCR-1). The same glance also shows a headshot, "Recife, Brazil" and a bold "**Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)**" line, so the first things a screener notices besides the stack are the cost and risk of hiring from abroad (SCR-8, SEN-6). Lower down, "English: Intermediate" and star-rated skills add doubt, and an undecided screener rejects (SCR-15). Likely kept reading, but at risk on the first pass.

**Target and weighting:** From the request: Senior Java Engineer, about 10 years, applying from Brazil to employers in Germany and Austria, needs visa sponsorship. No job description given, so RED-6, SKL-11 and ATS-1 could not be checked against a job ad. Match: JA medium-high (hiring manager for engineers, UK product company, not DACH and not Java-specific), TW medium-high (UK and Europe, includes finance clients), JB medium-high (EMEA, cross-border hires), SB low (UK JavaScript and TypeScript startups). No recruiter is a high match, so no Single source rule was raised. REG-5 rests on SB alone (low match) but is already at Low, the floor. No other severity was adjusted. No recruiter in the evidence hires in DACH; region findings below carry that caveat.

## Findings

### High
- **REG-6** (Supported 2, High): the languages line says "English: Intermediate". A screener reads that as below the working English needed to follow technical requirements, and one recruiter reports rejecting on it. Fix: if your English is at a working level (you use it in meetings, specs and code review), state it that way truthfully, or drop the line on an English CV (REG-9, Single source 1); if it is not yet at that level, that is a real gap to close, not a wording issue (RED-3). DACH conventions may differ (some teams work in German) and no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **STR-3** (Consensus 3, High): the skills line uses star ratings, "Java ★★★★★ | Spring Boot ★★★★★ | ... | AWS ★★☆☆☆". Skill graphics slow skimming and add nothing, and two-star items invite the reader to discount them. Fix: replace with a plain, grouped list (see Rewrites).
- **ACH-1** (Consensus 4, High): the Lojamar Commerce role has one duty-style bullet, "Built checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring.", with no problem, personal contribution or result. The most recent role passes this check; the oldest role is fine kept short (STR-1). Fix: say what the APIs served, what you personally did and what changed.
- **ACH-6** (Consensus 3, High): only the Banco Arvoredo bullets carry numbers. "Built checkout and inventory APIs ..." and "Maintained Java EE applications for insurance clients." have none. Fix: add real figures with placeholders such as [N orders a day], [X% faster checkout], [N applications] and fill them only with numbers you can defend.
- **SKL-4** (Supported 2, High): nothing on the CV shows how AI coding tools are used in daily work. One recruiter reports rejecting on this. Fix: if you use AI tools in development, add one concrete bullet on how (which tool, for what task, what it changed). Do not add it if it is not true.
- **ACH-4** (Consensus 3, High): no side projects, open source or hackathons are listed. Fix: if you have relevant ones (Java, Spring, Kafka or payments work outside the job), add a short Projects section with dates and context (STR-15). If you have none, skip this rather than invent one.

### Medium
- **REG-7** (Single source 1, Medium): "[Photo: professional headshot, top right]". TW reports rejecting CVs with photos. Fix: remove the photo. DACH conventions may differ (photos are more common on German CVs) and no DACH recruiter is in the evidence; the rule is kept at its weighted severity.
- **REG-3** (Supported 2, Medium): the header has an email but no phone number. Fix: add your real phone number with its country code, [+55 phone number]. DACH conventions may differ and no DACH recruiter is in the evidence. See REG-17 below.
- **SKL-3** (Supported 2, Medium): "Docker", "Kubernetes", "AWS" and "PostgreSQL" appear only in the skills line; the reader cannot tell in which role they were used. Fix: name each one in the bullet of the role where you used it, or drop it from the skills list.
- **SKL-10** (Single source 1, Medium): no testing tools are named anywhere. One recruiter reports rejecting on this. Fix: name the one or two testing tools you used and how, for example in a Banco Arvoredo bullet: "[testing tools] for [what was tested]".
- **STR-4** (Supported 2, Medium): the profile is one sentence, "Senior Java engineer with 10 years of experience in banking and e-commerce systems.", which says what you are but not your specialism or strongest result. Fix: two or three lines with the core stack and the best result already on the CV (ACH-10, Single source 1). See Rewrites.
- **STR-16** (Single source 1, Medium): the profile opens "Senior Java engineer", which ties you to one language; one recruiter reports rejecting on this. The header title "Senior Software Engineer (Java)" already gets the balance right. Fix: open the profile with "Senior software engineer" and name Java as the specialism.

### Low
- **REG-2** (Single source 1, Low): "**Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)**" sits in bold under the name. JB advises not stating a need for sponsorship on the CV; it is the first thing a screener notices after the title. Fix: delete the line and state the location and relocation plan instead (REG-5, REG-14). The sponsorship question comes up on the first call. If you hold any existing right to work in the EU, state that plainly instead (REG-15, Single source 1). DACH conventions may differ and no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **REG-5** (Single source 1, Low): the CV says "Recife, Brazil" but not that you will relocate. Fix: add "relocating to Germany or Austria" next to the city. DACH conventions may differ and no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **SKL-17** (Single source 1, Low): the stars rate each skill's level. Fix: covered by the STR-3 rewrite; list skills without levels.
- **SKL-8** (Single source 1, Low): the skills line is one flat list. Fix: group it (languages, frameworks, data and messaging, infrastructure).
- **STR-24** (Single source 1, Low): role dates give years only, "2020 to present", "2018 to 2020". Fix: add start and end months, [Month 2020] to present.
- **STR-7** (Single source 1, Low): no role opens with company context. Fix: one or two lines under Banco Arvoredo and Lojamar Commerce on what the company does, the team and the main problem, using facts you can state.
- **CHN-5** (Single source 1, Low): no LinkedIn URL in the header. Fix: add [LinkedIn URL].

### Judgment calls
- **REG-16** (Contested 1-1): for: TW says location filters and client ATS systems screen out applicants from abroad, and reports rejecting on it (TW-59, TW-60); against: JA says candidates blame location when skill gaps are the real cause (JA-40). Recommendation: fix the High findings first, then state "Recife, Brazil, relocating to Germany or Austria" in one line so location is a known fact, not a surprise. No DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **REG-17** (Contested 2-1): for: SB and TW say screeners or ATS systems notice a foreign phone number (SB-39, TW-60); against: JA says screeners do not know dialling codes (JA-104). Recommendation: Low. Use your real number with the +55 code (REG-3); do not list a German or Austrian number you do not have (RED-3).

## Rewrites

**Header**
- Before: "Senior Software Engineer (Java) | Recife, Brazil | [email]" and "**Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)**"
- After: "Senior Software Engineer (Java) | Recife, Brazil, relocating to Germany or Austria | [email] | [+55 phone number] | [LinkedIn URL]"

**Profile**
- Before: "Senior Java engineer with 10 years of experience in banking and e-commerce systems."
- After: "Senior software engineer specializing in Java and Spring Boot, with 10 years building banking and e-commerce systems. Built payment microservices handling 2 million transactions a day and led the Kafka migration of a card authorization service that cut latency by 38%."

**Lojamar Commerce bullet**
- Before: "Built checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring."
- After: "Built the checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring for [what the platform sold, N orders a day], [result, such as checkout errors down by X%]."

**Tecnorte Sistemas bullet**
- Before: "Maintained Java EE applications for insurance clients."
- After: "Maintained [N] Java EE applications for [N] insurance clients, [one result, such as fewer production incidents or faster releases]."

**Skills**
- Before: "Java ★★★★★ | Spring Boot ★★★★★ | Kafka ★★★★☆ | PostgreSQL ★★★★☆ | Docker ★★★☆☆ | Kubernetes ★★☆☆☆ | AWS ★★☆☆☆"
- After: "Languages: Java 17 | Frameworks: Spring Boot, Java EE | Data and messaging: Kafka, PostgreSQL | Infrastructure: Docker, Kubernetes, AWS | Testing: [tools you used]"

**Languages**
- Before: "Portuguese: Native | English: Intermediate | German: A1"
- After: "Portuguese: Native | English: [working level, only if accurate] | German: A1"

## What already works
- The most recent role shows the target's core stack and kind of problem at the top of the experience section, in reverse date order (SCR-2, SCR-7, Consensus 3).
- The Banco Arvoredo bullets show contribution and measurable results, "2 million transactions a day" and "latency fell by 38%" (ACH-1, Consensus 4; ACH-6, Consensus 3), and 38% is not a rounded figure (ACH-8, Single source 1).
- "Led migration of the card authorization service" shows ownership (ACH-12, Single source 1).
- The current city is stated truthfully in the header (REG-14, Consensus 3).
- The CV is focused: one page, older roles cut to one line (STR-1, Consensus 3).
- Role headings lead with the job title and give the employer's location (STR-14, STR-19, Single source 1); each role names its domain, banking, e-commerce, insurance (ACH-2, Single source 1).
- Education names the university and degree (STR-27, Supported 2).
- No gaps between roles (GAP-2, GAP-3), no first-person pronouns (STR-17), no em-dashes (RED-5).
- "German: A1" shows you are learning the local language; keep it, but it is not presented as a working skill, which is right (REG-10, Single source 1).

## Beyond the CV
- **CHN-15** (Consensus 3): ask for referrals into DACH companies; a referred CV gets a more careful read, which matters for a candidate from abroad.
- **CHN-17** (Supported 2): with each application, send the hiring manager a short message on why you fit the role.
- **CHN-16** (Supported 2): network alongside applications rather than relying only on cold applications.
- **CHN-18** (Consensus 3) and **CHN-20** (Single source 1): keep LinkedIn complete and current, with the same roles and dates as the CV; **CHN-10** (Single source 1): use a standard title such as "Senior Software Engineer" in the headline.

## Not covered by the evidence
- No recruiter in the evidence hires in DACH. Local conventions (photo, German-format CV, expected German level, Austrian versus German employer practice) are not covered. General advice, not from the evidence: check a few target employers' job ads for whether they work in English and ask for a photo or German CV.
- Visa routes (EU Blue Card in Germany, Red-White-Red Card in Austria), salary thresholds and sponsorship timing are out of scope for the CV rules. General advice: raise sponsorship on the first call, not on the CV.
- No job description was given, so keyword match (ATS-1), the share of listed skills shown (SKL-11, RED-6) and cloud-provider fit (SKL-5) were not checked. Run this review again against a specific job ad.
