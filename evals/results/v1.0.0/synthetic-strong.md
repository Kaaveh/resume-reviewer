# CV review: Senior Android Engineer for Germany (relocating from Serbia)

**Verdict:** Invite

**10-second verdict:** The top of page one shows the title "Senior Android Engineer", the stack "Kotlin and Jetpack Compose", 8 years of experience and a current senior role at Pulsefield Health, a Berlin company, with a 3 million user fitness app. A screener checking for a senior Android profile with the right stack finds it at once (SCR-1, SCR-2, SCR-7, SCR-6) and keeps reading. Location ("Belgrade, Serbia (relocating to Germany)") is stated up front, so it is a known fact, not a surprise.

**Target and weighting:** Senior Android Engineer, about 8 years, applying to German employers from outside the EU and relocating; from the request, and close to the default target (`android-eu-relocation`), so the default match levels apply. No job description was given, so hard requirements were checked against a typical senior Android stack. Match: JA high, TW medium-high, JB medium-high, SB low-medium. No recruiter in the evidence hires in DACH, so German conventions may differ from every finding below.

## Findings

### Medium
- **SKL-3** (Supported 2, Medium): several skills in the list cannot be tied to a role. "Coroutines, Flow, ... Hilt, Retrofit", "MockK", "JUnit", "GitHub Actions" and "Firebase Crashlytics" appear only in the Skills section, and the Kestrel Mobility role names no language or libraries at all ("Rebuilt the driver app's location tracking with a foreground service"). A reader cannot tell where these were used, and the Kestrel stack is not easy to find (SCR-7). Fix: add a one-line stack under each of the two recent roles, naming only what was really used there (see Rewrites; this also fits the SKL-2 recommendation). DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **STR-17** (Single source 1, JA hiring manager, raised from Low to Medium): the Pulsefield context paragraph uses the first person: "I joined the 6-person Android team to fix slow sync and crash rates." Raised one level because JA is the high match for this target and the CV is in the situation he described; it is a preference (0F/1P), so it will not cost an interview on its own. Fix: rephrase without "I" (see Rewrites). DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.

### Low
- **REG-15** (Single source 1, JB former recruiter, Low): the header states "relocating to Germany" but not the right to work in Germany or the visa route, so a screener cannot see the candidate's status. Balance this with **REG-2** (Single source 1, JB, Low): do not write that sponsorship is needed. Fix: if the candidate holds or qualifies for a German work permit, state that status in plain words in the header line, for example "[work permit status for Germany]"; if nothing is in place yet, keep the relocation line as it is and leave sponsorship off the CV. DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.

### Judgment calls
- **REG-16** (Contested 1-1): for: TW says location filters and client ATS systems screen out applicants from abroad (TW-59, TW-60); against: JA says candidates blame location when skill gaps are the real cause (JA-40). Recommendation: no change. The CV has no skill gap for the role, and it already states the real city (REG-14) and relocation in one line (REG-5), which is what the guidance asks for.
- **SKL-2** (Contested 1-1): for: JB says do not repeat the stack under each role (JB-22); against: JA says a stack line under each job is good when there is space (JA-73). Recommendation: add one short stack line per recent role (JA, the high match), not a full list repeated everywhere. This fixes SKL-3 above.
- **STR-2** (Contested range 4): JA wants 1 page for 6 to 10 years; TW, JB and SB accept 2. Recommendation: the CV is about one page, which fits every recruiter's range for 8 years. Keep it at one page, or two at most after adding the stack lines.
