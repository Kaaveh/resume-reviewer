# CV review: Senior Java Engineer for employers in Germany and Austria (DACH), visa sponsorship needed

**Verdict:** Borderline

**10-second verdict:** The top of page one shows a photo, the title "Senior Software Engineer (Java)", the location "Recife, Brazil", a bold line "Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)", and a most recent role at a bank with "Java 17 and Spring Boot handling 2 million transactions a day". The core stack and a strong recent result are visible at once (SCR-1, SCR-2, SCR-7), so a screener would likely keep reading. What could stop them is not the skills but the presentation around them: the sponsorship line in bold before any experience, the star ratings in the skills section and "English: Intermediate" further down. This is one screener's likely view, not a certainty (SCR-16).

**Target and weighting:** From the request: Senior Java Engineer, about 10 years, applying to employers in Germany and Austria from Brazil, needing visa sponsorship. No job description was given, so hard requirements are judged from the role title (Java, Spring). Match levels used: JA medium-high (hiring manager for engineers at a tech product company, but UK only), TW medium-high (agency, UK and Europe, tech and finance), JB medium-high (former recruiter, EMEA and cross-border hires), SB low (UK JavaScript and TypeScript startups). No recruiter in the evidence hires in DACH, so every finding below may differ from German and Austrian conventions.

## Findings

No Critical findings. The core stack is present: Java, Spring Boot and Kafka are shown in the most recent role, so RED-2 passes.

### High
- **STR-3** (Consensus 3, High): The skills section rates every skill with stars ("Java ★★★★★ | Spring Boot ★★★★★ ... Kubernetes ★★☆☆☆ | AWS ★★☆☆☆"). Skill bars slow skimming and add nothing for the reader. JA also says not to label proficiency levels (SKL-17, Single source 1, Low), and two stars on Kubernetes and AWS reads as a skill labelled basic (SKL-15, Single source 1, Low). Fix: replace the ratings with a plain, grouped list (SKL-8); drop or move to a lower line any tool the candidate could not defend in an interview (RED-3). DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **REG-6** (Supported 2, High): "English: Intermediate" tells the reader English may not be at a working level, and one recruiter reports rejecting on this. For a candidate abroad applying to international teams in Germany and Austria, English is often the working language. Fix: if the candidate works in English day to day (for example in code reviews, documentation or meetings), state that level truthfully, such as "English: professional working proficiency"; never claim a level the candidate cannot show in an interview (RED-3). JA adds that listing English on an English CV adds nothing (REG-9, Single source 1, Low), so removing the line is also an option once the CV itself shows English in use. DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **ACH-1** (Consensus 4, High): The two older roles describe duties, not problems and results: "Built checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring." and "Maintained Java EE applications for insurance clients." The most recent role already does this well. Fix: for the Lojamar role, say what the checkout and inventory work changed for the business and what the candidate personally owned; keep the Tecnorte role short (STR-1) but name one outcome.
- **ACH-6** (Consensus 3, High): Only the most recent role carries numbers; "Lojamar Commerce" and "Tecnorte Sistemas" have none. A hire from abroad has to look stronger than the team average (SEN-6, Single source 1, context), so measured results matter more here. Fix: add one real figure per older role using placeholders below; the candidate supplies the actual numbers.
- **SKL-4** (Supported 2, High): The CV says nothing about using AI coding tools in daily work, and one recruiter reports rejecting on this. TW adds that hiring managers want some exposure to AI tools (SKL-7, Single source 1, Low). Fix: if the candidate uses AI tools at work, add one concrete line under the Banco Arvoredo role (which tool, for what task). If not, leave it out rather than invent it.
- **ACH-4** (Consensus 3, High): No side projects or hackathons are listed. Fix: if the candidate has a relevant Java or backend side project or hackathon, add a short Projects section with dates and context (STR-15). If there is none, skip it; do not create one for the CV.

### Medium
- **REG-7** (Single source 1, Medium): "[Photo: professional headshot, top right]". TW says no photo or pictures of any kind and reports rejecting on it. Kept at Medium: TW is a medium-high, not high, match. Fix: remove the photo for applications in English. Note: photos are common on German-language CVs and in DACH generally; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence, so this rule may not reflect local practice there.
- **SKL-3** (Supported 2, Medium): "PostgreSQL", "Docker", "Kubernetes" and "AWS" appear only in the skills list; no role says where they were used. Fix: name each in the role where it was used, in one short stack line per role (accepted per SKL-2 guidance), or drop the ones with no real use.
- **SKL-10** (Single source 1, Medium): No testing tools are named anywhere (for example JUnit, Mockito, Testcontainers). JA reports rejecting on this. Kept at Medium: JA is a medium-high match here. Fix: name the one or two testing tools the candidate actually used and how, in the Banco Arvoredo role.

### Low
- **REG-2** (Single source 1, Low): "**Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)**" is stated in bold in the header. JB advises not stating a need for sponsorship on the CV; JB also says to make right-to-work status obvious (REG-15, Single source 1, Low), and JA says bold only what should catch the eye, such as a strong result (STR-23, Single source 1, Low). Fix: remove the bold sponsorship line; state the city and readiness to relocate instead (REG-5, REG-14), and give the visa position in the application form or on the first call. JB is a medium-high match for cross-border hires, so this is worth doing even at Low. DACH conventions may differ; no DACH recruiter is in the evidence.
- **REG-5** (Single source 1, Low): The CV gives the location "Recife, Brazil" but does not say the candidate will relocate. Fix: add "Open to relocation to Germany or Austria" to the header line.
- **REG-3** (Supported 2, lowered from Medium to Low): The header has an email but no phone number. Lowered because both supporters gave preferences only and their UK context differs from DACH. Fix: add the candidate's real phone number with its country code (+55); do not suggest a local number the candidate does not have (RED-3).
- **CHN-5** (Single source 1, Low): No LinkedIn URL in the header. Fix: add it, and make sure the profile matches the CV (CHN-20, see Beyond the CV).
- **SKL-8** (Single source 1, Low): The skills list is one flat line. Fix: group it (languages and frameworks, messaging and data, infrastructure).
- **STR-24** (Single source 1, Low): Dates give years only ("2020 to present", "2018 to 2020"). Fix: add start and end months.
- **STR-7** (Single source 1, Low): No role opens with company context. Fix: one or two lines under Banco Arvoredo on what the bank does, its size and the team (STR-8: 2 to 4 lines at most).
- **ACH-7** (Single source 1, Low): Product scale is shown ("2 million transactions a day") but not team size. Fix: add team size, such as "[N] engineers", where it helps.
- **STR-21** (Single source 1, Low): The profile is one line; JA suggests 3 to 4 lines. Fix: extend it with the specialism (payments, event-driven systems) and why the candidate fits the target role (STR-4), using facts from the CV only.

### Judgment calls
- **REG-16** (Contested 1-1): For: TW says location filters and client ATS systems screen out applicants from abroad (TW-59, TW-60). Against: JA says candidates blame location when skill gaps are the real cause (JA-40). Recommendation: fix the skill-presentation findings first (JA), then state the real city and readiness to relocate in one header line (REG-14, REG-5), so location is a known fact rather than a surprise. A bold sponsorship banner makes location and visa the first thing read, which is the opposite of this advice.

## Rewrites

**Header line**
- Before: "Senior Software Engineer (Java) | Recife, Brazil | [email]" followed by "**Requires visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card)**"
- After: "Senior Software Engineer (Java) | Recife, Brazil, open to relocation to Germany or Austria | [email] | [phone with +55 code] | [LinkedIn URL]"

**Profile**
- Before: "Senior Java engineer with 10 years of experience in banking and e-commerce systems."
- After: "Senior Java engineer with 10 years of experience in banking and e-commerce systems. Builds payment microservices in Java 17 and Spring Boot handling 2 million transactions a day, and led the migration of a card authorization service to Kafka that cut latency by 38%. [One line on why the candidate fits the target employer's product or domain.]"

**Lojamar Commerce bullet**
- Before: "Built checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring."
- After: "Built checkout and inventory APIs in Java and Spring serving [N orders a day], [result, such as checkout errors down by X% or a new capability shipped]."

**Tecnorte Sistemas bullet**
- Before: "Maintained Java EE applications for insurance clients."
- After: "Maintained Java EE applications for [N] insurance clients, [one outcome, such as reduced incidents by X% or delivered Y]."

**Skills**
- Before: "Java ★★★★★ | Spring Boot ★★★★★ | Kafka ★★★★☆ | PostgreSQL ★★★★☆ | Docker ★★★☆☆ | Kubernetes ★★☆☆☆ | AWS ★★☆☆☆"
- After: "Languages and frameworks: Java 17, Spring Boot, Spring, Java EE | Messaging and data: Kafka, PostgreSQL | Infrastructure: Docker[, Kubernetes, AWS if used in real work] | Testing: [tools actually used]"

**Languages**
- Before: "Portuguese: Native | English: Intermediate | German: A1"
- After: "Portuguese: native | English: [truthful working level, such as professional working proficiency] | German: A1 (learning)"

## What already works
- The most recent role leads the experience section, in reverse date order, and shows the core stack and the kind of problem a senior Java role solves (SCR-2, SCR-7).
- The top role has measured, non-round results: "2 million transactions a day", "latency fell by 38%" (ACH-6, ACH-8), and "Led migration" shows ownership (ACH-12).
- Each role names the domain: banking, e-commerce, insurance (ACH-2), which helps finance employers looking for industry experience (SCR-10).
- Role headings lead with the job title and give the employer's location (STR-14, STR-19); employer names are easy to spot (SCR-6).
- The current city is stated truthfully (REG-14).
- Education names the university and degree, placed at the end (STR-27, STR-26).
- The CV is short and focused, older roles are cut to one line (STR-1), and there are no gaps (GAP-3).
- German A1 is listed as it is, without overstating it (REG-10: it shows learning but is not a working skill).

## Beyond the CV
- **CHN-15** (Consensus 3): Ask for referrals at target employers in Germany and Austria; a referral gets the CV read more carefully, which matters for a candidate who needs sponsorship.
- **CHN-16** (Supported 2): Network alongside applications rather than relying only on cold applications.
- **CHN-17** (Supported 2): Send the hiring manager a short message saying why the candidate fits the role.
- **CHN-18** (Consensus 3) and **CHN-20** (Single source 1, TW): Keep LinkedIn complete and consistent with the CV (photo, full job history with dates, what was done at each job, a bio).
- **CHN-10** (Single source 1, TW): Use a standard LinkedIn headline such as "Senior Software Engineer (Java)".

## Not covered by the evidence
- **DACH conventions.** No recruiter in the evidence hires in Germany or Austria. Photos, German-format CVs (Lebenslauf), date of birth and expectations about German language level are not covered; the photo and language findings above are UK and EMEA views.
- **German language requirement.** Whether German A1 is enough depends on the employer; many international tech teams in Berlin, Munich or Vienna work in English, while others require German. General advice: check each job ad and say in applications if the candidate is studying German.
- **Visa routes.** Whether the candidate qualifies for an EU Blue Card (Germany) or Red-White-Red Card (Austria) is out of scope for the evidence. General advice: check eligibility on official government sites before claiming it on the CV or in applications.
- **Hard requirements without a job description.** No job ad was provided, so SKL-11 (80 percent of listed skills) and ATS-1 (keywords in the job's wording) could not be checked. Rerun the review against a specific ad.
