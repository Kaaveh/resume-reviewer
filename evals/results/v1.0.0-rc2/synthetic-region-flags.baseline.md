# CV review: Mateus Ferreira-Lindqvist

Target: Senior Java Engineer, employers in Germany and Austria, needs visa sponsorship (EU Blue Card).

## Verdict: borderline

The core is strong for the target. Ten years of Java, recent Java 17 and Spring Boot, Kafka, and two quantified results in a regulated domain (banking payments) are exactly what DACH Java hiring managers look for. A company that already sponsors and works in English would read this CV with interest.

Two things keep it from an invite. First, "English: Intermediate" with German A1 means the candidate cannot show a working language for the job. For a sponsored senior hire that is often the first screen. Second, the CV is too thin to justify the cost and delay of sponsorship: one line per older role, a one-sentence profile, and skill star ratings instead of evidence. Fix the language line (if the real level is higher) and add depth, and this becomes an invite for English-speaking teams.

## Problems by severity

### Critical

1. **Language level undermines the application.** "English: Intermediate | German: A1" tells a recruiter the candidate may not be able to work in either language. Senior roles involve design discussions, incident calls and mentoring. Most sponsoring DACH tech employers work in English and expect B2 to C1 at minimum; German-speaking teams (common in Austrian and German banks and insurers) expect B2+ German. If the real English level is higher, state it in CEFR terms (for example "English: C1, daily working language with international teams"). If it really is intermediate, this is a skills gap to close before applying, not a CV fix.

2. **Not enough evidence to justify sponsorship.** A sponsored hire costs the employer time and paperwork, so the CV has to make the candidate clearly worth it. Right now only the current role has real content. The 2018 to 2020 and 2016 to 2018 roles have one generic bullet each, and there is nothing on scope (team size, ownership, on-call, architecture decisions, mentoring) that signals "senior".

### Major

3. **Skill star ratings.** ★★★★★ scales mean nothing to a reader, invite doubt ("five stars in Java, compared to whom?") and are not parsed by ATS systems. Two stars in Kubernetes and AWS also advertises weakness in tools many DACH job ads list. Replace with a plain grouped list and show depth in the experience bullets.

4. **Profile is one generic line.** "Senior Java engineer with 10 years of experience in banking and e-commerce systems" does not say what the candidate is best at or what they want. Use it to state the specialism, the strongest result, the working language and the relocation/visa position in one place.

5. **Visa line is framed as a need rather than a fact.** Stating the visa situation openly is correct; hiding it wastes everyone's time. But "Requires visa sponsorship" in bold at the top reads as the headline. Reframe it as eligibility: a BSc plus a senior Java salary normally qualifies for the EU Blue Card, and IT specialists can also qualify via experience. Something like "Eligible for EU Blue Card; available to relocate within X months" shifts the message from cost to readiness.

6. **No dates at month level and no links.** "2020 to present" hides gaps and tenure; DACH recruiters expect MM/YYYY. There is no phone number, LinkedIn or GitHub. For a remote, cross-border candidate, LinkedIn is often the first thing checked.

### Minor

7. **Photo.** A professional photo is still common and accepted in Germany and Austria, so it is not an error here. It is optional, though, and many international tech companies prefer CVs without one. Keep it only if it is a clean, professional headshot; drop it for companies with anonymised or US-style processes.

8. **Older roles lack technology detail.** "Java and Spring" and "Java EE" should name versions and stack (Spring Boot version, JPA/Hibernate, REST, testing tools) so keyword matching works.

9. **Location line could show intent.** "Recife, Brazil" alone signals distance. Add "relocating to Germany/Austria" so the reader does not have to infer it.

10. **Education line is fine** but could add the degree's equivalence status (anabin "H+" for the university, if applicable), which helps Blue Card processing and reassures HR.
