## Summary

12 claims checked: 11 agree, 1 disagree (SB-47). Every claim is spoken by the guest, every stance is right, every episode is right, and no measure claim was in the set. Eight of the agreements carry nits (SB-03, SB-11, SB-17, SB-24, SB-37, SB-39, SB-40, SB-41), mostly wording that `claim_en` takes from outside the anchor or hedges it drops.

Patterns worth adding to the extraction guide:

- **Mixed types across anchors.** The guide says to split a sentence that mixes advice and the recruiter's reaction; SB-47 shows the same mix spread over two anchors of one claim. The rule could read: if the anchors of one claim differ in type (P advice and F! rejection), make two claims.
- **Everything in `claim_en` must be in an anchor.** In SB-03 ("build relationships"), SB-37 (LinkedIn and GitHub URLs, already on SB-28) and SB-40 ("and on LinkedIn") the claim says more than its quotes carry. Either extend the quote (within three sentences) or drop the extra.
- **The answer word sits before the quote.** In SB-39 the guest's "yeah actually I do" is what makes the claim a statement about checking the phone number; the quote should start at the answer, not after it. Same family as the S004 finding on scope words before the window.
- **Hedges still drop.** "you don't need to" became "Do not" (SB-17), "i think most recruiters" lost "i think" (SB-41), "I guess" was dropped (SB-11).
- **Compound rules counted from one part.** REG-1 (location and right to work), REG-4 (phone country and distance) and CHN-2 (complete, active, consistent with the CV) get SB's support from one clause each. That passes a lenient reading of the strict test, but the tally then shows SB behind clauses SB never stated (for example right to work in REG-1). Consider splitting REG-1 into a location rule and a right-to-work rule, or letting a claim record which part of a compound rule it supports.
